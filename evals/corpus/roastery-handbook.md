# Lantern Bay Roastery Handbook (self-written fictional document)

This document was written for evaluation purposes. The roastery, its staff and all figures are invented and unrelated to any real company.

## 1. About the roastery

Lantern Bay Roastery is an imaginary small-batch coffee roaster with one shop and a roasting room in the back. It was founded in 2017 by two friends and employs nine people. The roastery sells whole beans online and through the shop, and it never sells pre-ground coffee because ground coffee goes stale within days.

The roasting room is open to staff only. Visitors may watch through a glass window on Saturdays between 10 a.m. and noon, when public tasting sessions take place.

## 2. Green coffee sourcing

Lantern Bay buys green coffee from six partner farms. Four of them are in Colombia, one is in Ethiopia and one is in Guatemala. Contracts are renewed every October, and the roastery pays a fixed premium of 0.40 dollars per pound above the market price for lots that score at least 86 points in cupping.

Green coffee is stored in a warehouse kept at 18 degrees Celsius and 60 percent relative humidity. Bags are stacked on pallets, never directly on the floor, and a lot is used within ten months of arrival. Every incoming bag is weighed and a sample of 350 grams is saved for quality checks.

## 3. The roasting process

The main roaster is a 15-kilogram drum machine. A normal batch uses 12 kilograms of green coffee, because loading it to full capacity makes the heat uneven. The drum is preheated to 200 degrees Celsius before beans go in, and the charge temperature drop is recorded as the turning point.

A light roast for Ethiopian beans lasts about nine minutes and ends at a drop temperature near 198 degrees. The medium roast used for the house blend takes eleven minutes and drops at 207 degrees. Dark roasts are rarely made, and the darkest product, the Harbor Night blend, stops before the beans become oily.

The roaster watches for first crack, which usually begins around 196 degrees. The development time ratio, the share of total time after first crack, is kept between 15 and 20 percent for filter coffees. After the drop, beans are cooled in the cooling tray for four minutes until they reach room temperature.

## 4. Quality control

After roasting, every batch is tested for color with a spectrometer. The accepted color reading for the house blend lies between 58 and 62 on the agtron scale. A batch outside this range is set aside and cannot be sold as the house blend.

Beans rest for at least two days before packing, because freshly roasted coffee releases too much carbon dioxide. Cupping happens every weekday at 2 p.m. with at least three tasters. A batch is rejected if two tasters mark the same defect, such as a baked or sour flavor.

## 5. Packaging and shelf life

Beans are packed in one-way valve bags in sizes of 250 grams and 1 kilogram. Each bag is labeled with the roast date rather than a best-before date. The recommended window for drinking is between one and five weeks after the roast date, and the shop removes unsold bags from the shelf after six weeks.

Nitrogen flushing is not used. The owners decided that the extra cost of 0.15 dollars per bag did not give a noticeable benefit given how fast beans sell. Packaging film is recyclable, and customers receive a 5 percent discount when they bring back an empty bag.

## 6. Brewing recommendations

For filter coffee, the shop recommends a ratio of 1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water and a water temperature of 93 degrees Celsius. A pour-over should take roughly three minutes. For espresso, the shop uses 18 grams of coffee to pull 36 grams of liquid in about 28 seconds.

Water quality matters too. The staff aims for total dissolved solids between 75 and 150 parts per million. Very soft water makes coffee taste flat, and very hard water produces scale in machines and a chalky taste.

## 7. Equipment maintenance

The roaster's chaff collector is emptied after every batch to prevent fires. The drum and exhaust duct are cleaned every Friday evening, and a professional technician inspects the burners twice a year, in March and September. The grinders in the shop are recalibrated every morning before opening.

Espresso machines are backflushed with detergent once a week and descaled every three months. The water filter cartridges are replaced every six months, or after 2,000 liters, whichever comes first.

## 8. Safety rules

Staff must wear hearing protection in the roasting room when the cooling fan runs, because noise reaches 85 decibels. A fire extinguisher is mounted next to the roaster, and every employee completes fire training once a year. Chaff is a fire risk, so it is put into a metal bin with a lid and carried outside at the end of each day.

## 9. Customer service

Online orders placed before 1 p.m. on weekdays are roasted and shipped within two business days. Customers can return unopened bags within 14 days for a full refund. If a customer reports a taste problem, the roastery replaces the bag once without asking for proof, and logs the roast date to check for a pattern.
